Ten questions

Questions & answers

The short version of how Ready Up works, and how you're looked after.

01

Who owns what we make here?

The people who make it. A project belongs to its team, everyone who worked on it, not to Ready Up. We never take ownership of your work or your ideas.

02

What happens to my work?

It's yours to show, always. Everything you contribute lands on your Ready Up profile: a living, shareable page with your CV, links, reels, and credited projects all in one place, that you can point a recruiter, collaborator, or friend straight at. You keep the right to show any of it, with credit, during the project and long after, even if you leave or the project winds down.

03

Who can see my profile?

You decide. Make it public, keep it to the community, or hold parts back, which is handy if you're job-hunting quietly and don't want it broadcast. Your profile, your call.

04

What kind of projects can we build?

Not only games. That's the heart of it: games, animations, software, tools, a wild experiment... if it's an idea worth building and you can gather the right people for it, it belongs here.

05

Is it really free?

Yes, free for everyone making things. No fees, no catch. If Ready Up grows enough to need funding, it'll come from partners and referral links, not from charging the people building here.

06

Do I have to be unemployed to join?

Not at all. Between roles, between contracts, working and building on the side, or just good at what you do and wanting to use it. All welcome.

07

How much time does it take?

As much or as little as you like. Fit it around your job hunt or day job, pick projects that match your capacity, and step back whenever you need. No lock-in, no pressure.

08

What if I get a job partway through a project?

That's the whole point — go get it. Give your team a heads-up, and before you step away, leave things tidy: notes, files, and enough documentation for whoever picks up where you left off, so the project keeps moving without hiccups. You keep all your credit and the right to show what you made. Projects are built to carry on when people move on.

09

What if a project takes off?

Nice problem to have. If something gains real traction, whether that's a release, funding, or actual money, the team pauses and agrees fair terms together, in good faith, before going further. Nobody's contribution gets forgotten.

10

What's not allowed here?

Be decent, be legal, and only share work that's yours. No hate speech, harassment, or shock content; nothing that endangers minors; no stolen or NDA-bound material; no malware or spam. Mature themes in the things you build are fine. Being harmful to real people isn't. The full details are in our Community Guidelines.

Didn't find your answer?

Join today and come ask in the Discord. Someone will be glad to help, and you'll meet the people building here.
