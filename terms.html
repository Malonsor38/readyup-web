Terms of Service

This is the agreement between you and Ready Up. It is written to be read, not to be impenetrable — but it is still the thing that applies if something goes wrong, so it is worth ten minutes.

The short version: you own what you make, we host it, and neither of us should be a problem for the other. The Community Guidelines cover behaviour, and they form part of these terms.

Who you're agreeing with

Ready Up is run by Miguel Alonso Requena, an individual based in Spain, reachable at malonsor038@gmail.com. There is no company behind it yet. If that changes, this section changes with it.

By creating an account or using the site, you accept these terms and the Community Guidelines. If you don't accept them, don't use Ready Up — that is the only consequence.

Who can join

  • You must be at least 16. Ready Up handles personal data, and 16 is the age at which you can consent to that yourself across the EU without a guardian. If you are younger, this is not the place for you yet.
  • One person, one account. Real identity, no duplicates, no accounts on someone else's behalf.
  • You must be allowed to be here. If your employment contract, NDA, or local law prevents you from doing collaborative creative work, that's between you and them — but don't bring the problem here.

Your account

You need a verified email address to join. Keep your password to yourself; you're responsible for what happens under your account, and you should tell us if you think someone else has got into it.

Your profile address — the readyup.quest/your-handle part — is yours while you hold it, not a permanent grant. Some handles are reserved, impersonation is not allowed, and we may reclaim an address that infringes someone else's name or is clearly being squatted. If you change your handle, the old one stops working immediately.

You choose who sees your profile — public, members only, or hidden. Nothing you write is published beyond that setting, and you can change it whenever you like.

Your work stays yours

We do not take ownership of anything you make. Not your projects, not your ideas, not your CV, not a single asset. It is yours before, during and after.

What we do need is narrow permission to actually run the thing. By uploading or posting content, you give us a non-exclusive, worldwide, royalty-free licence to store, copy and display it — but only for the purpose of operating Ready Up and only as far as your own visibility setting allows. A hidden profile is not displayed to anyone. We do not sell your work, licence it onward, or use it to advertise without asking you first.

That licence ends when you delete the content or your account, apart from two ordinary exceptions: backups take a while to cycle out, and anything a teammate has legitimately already seen or downloaded is out of our hands.

You also promise the obvious: that what you upload is yours to upload. No NDA material, no assets you don't hold rights to, nothing lifted from a past employer.

Projects and teams

A project belongs to its team — everyone who worked on it — and not to Ready Up. We provide the space, the credits and the links; we are not a party to whatever you agree between yourselves.

Sort out ownership early, in writing, between you. This matters more than anything else on this page. Ready Up records who was on a team and what they did; it does not decide who owns the code, who can ship it, or who gets paid. If a project gains real traction — a commercial release, funding, actual money — stop and agree fair terms together before going further. We will not arbitrate that, and we cannot.

You keep your credit. If you leave a project or it winds down, your contribution stays on your profile, with the dates you were there, and you keep the right to show your own work.

Team links are a convenience, not a security boundary. A project space holds links to tools we don't run — boards, repositories, drives. Removing someone from a team here does not remove them from those tools. That is the team's job, and it is worth doing promptly.

AI disclosure

If any part of what you contribute is AI-generated, you must disclose it — to your team, and on your profile where it applies. Your team has to approve its use before it goes into shared work. If a team would rather have no AI in their project, that is their call to respect.

The AI-use statement on your profile is self-declared. We don't verify it and we don't present it as verified. Declaring it falsely is a straightforward breach of these terms.

How to behave

The Community Guidelines are the full answer, and they form part of these terms. In summary: be decent, be legal, only share work that's yours.

Additionally, and specific to the site itself: don't try to break it, scrape it, overload it, probe it for weaknesses, or get at data that isn't yours. Don't automate account creation. If you find a security hole, tell us rather than using it — we will be grateful rather than difficult.

What belongs to us

The Ready Up name, the site's design, its code and its written content are ours. You are welcome to link to us, write about us, screenshot us, and criticise us. You are not welcome to clone the site and present it as Ready Up, or use the name in a way that suggests we are involved with something we aren't.

Free, and early

Ready Up is free for the people making things, and there is no plan to charge them. If it ever needs funding, that will come from partners and referrals rather than from members. If that ever changes, you will hear about it before it affects you.

It is also early, and run by one person. Be realistic about what that means: features will change, things will occasionally break, and there is no support team behind a ticketing system. The service is provided as it is, without warranties of any kind.

Keep your own copies of anything that matters. Your CV, your artwork, your files. Ready Up is somewhere to show your work, not the only place it should exist.

Ending things

You can leave whenever you like, with no notice and no reason given. Delete your account from your profile page.

We can suspend or remove an account that breaks these terms or the Community Guidelines. We aim to be fair and to weigh context: usually that means a warning, or removing the content, before anything heavier. Serious harm — child endangerment, credible threats, malware — skips straight to permanent removal and, where the law requires it, a report to the authorities.

If we ever have to shut Ready Up down, we will give reasonable notice and a way to export what is yours.
